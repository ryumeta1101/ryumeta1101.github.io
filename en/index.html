Biography

I am an undergraduate student at Hokkaido University, working in the Laboratory of Media Dynamics (Haseyama–Ogawa Lab). My research is on recommender systems. In particular, I work on financial recommendation, which aims to recommend assets that are both profitable and suited to each investor's preferences, and on evaluation methodology, which asks how recommender systems should be evaluated. I am also interested in artificial market simulation and in analyzing human preferences.

Research Interests

I study recommender systems, mainly in the following two areas.

Financial Recommendation

Recommending financial assets to investors while balancing relevance to their preferences (nDCG) with profitability (ROI, Sharpe ratio). I proposed Expert-Following Strategies, which recommend the assets bought by investors with high past returns, and DSER, a core–satellite re-ranking that lowers the correlation between recommended assets.

Evaluation Methodology for Recommender Systems

Examining whether the metrics reported in recommender-system evaluation actually measure different things. I quantify the overlap among commonly used accuracy metrics with effective dimensionality and analyze their redundancy and validity.
